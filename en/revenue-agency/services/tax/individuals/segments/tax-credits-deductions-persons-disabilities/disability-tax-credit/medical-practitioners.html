Information for medical practitioners

Medical practitioners need to complete and certify the effects of the impairment for individuals applying for the disability tax credit (DTC). This essential information allows us to assess a patient's eligibility, or continued eligibility, for the DTC.

Completing the DTC application

Applicants (the person with the impairment, their legal representative, or their legal guardian if the person is under 18) complete Part A of the DTC application, and medical practitioners complete Part B. Applicants may apply for the DTC using the digital form or the printed paper form.

Part B must only be completed by the medical practitioner who is familiar with the effects of the applicant's impairment. Applicants cannot complete Part B themselves.

Who can certify impairments in Part B

Several types of medical practitioners can complete and certify Part B of the DTC application. The type of medical practitioner who can certify the form depends on the impairment being assessed.

Medical practitioners who can certify impairments
Medical doctor All impairments
Nurse practitioner All impairments
Optometrist Vision
Audiologist Hearing
Occupational therapist Walking, feeding, dressing
Physiotherapist Walking
Psychologist Mental functions
Speech-language pathologist Speaking

How to assess patients

Medical practitioners do not need to determine in advance whether or not a patient is eligible for the DTC. You only need to describe and certify the facts of the patient's limitations.

The CRA bases its decision about eligibility on the information that medical practitioners provide in Part B of the DTC application.

Use your best professional judgment when filling out Part B.

You may base your assessment on:

  • The symptoms reported by the patient
  • Your knowledge of the patient's medical history
  • Your direct observation of the patient’s limitations
  • Your knowledge of the effects of the impairment(s) as they relate to the patient's medical condition

If you don’t have medical information on file, consider asking your patient to provide it to you so you are comfortable with what you are certifying. If you feel you cannot certify a limitation (restriction) in a particular category of impairment, let your patient know that and suggest they have a medical practitioner who knows them complete the form.

How to complete Part B

If the applicant has completed Part A online or by phone, they will get a reference number you can use to complete Part B online.

If the applicant has completed Part A on paper, you must complete Part B on the same paper form.

If the applicant has not completed Part A yet, you can complete Part B on paper or online and print out the form for them to complete Part A.

Online
  1. Open the digital DTC application for medical practitioners
  2. Enter the information required

    • If you have a reference number: Option 1

      The reference number is valid until you submit Part B. The reference number, last name, and date of birth must match what was submitted in Part A.

      1. Enter the reference number provided by your patient.
      2. Answer a few questions about your patient and the impairment(s):
        • Basic information about you
        • Patient name and date of birth
        • Information about the effects of the impairment(s)
      3. After Part B is completed, the digital form is automatically submitted to the CRA
        • If the application is successfully submitted

          A confirmation page with a green checkmark will appear and you will have the option to save a copy of Part B for your records. Downloading a copy of the form for your records is optional but recommended.

        • If the application could not be submitted

          An alert page with an orange alert symbol (exclamation mark in a triangle), will appear if your patient’s DTC application could not be submitted to the CRA. If this happens,

          Complete the form manually
          1. Download a PDF copy of the T2201 form you filled out within 25 minutes
          2. Print and sign the last page of the T2201 form to certify it
          3. Give the form to your patient so they may fill out Part A and mail the completed T2201 form to the CRA

    • If you do not have a reference number: Option 2

      1. Fill out Part B of the form using the Digital application for medical practitioners
      2. Print out the form and sign it
      3. Give the form to your patient
      4. Your patient must then fill out Part A on the paper form and submit the completed form T2201 to the CRA by mail.

On paper
  1. Answer a few questions and completes only the impairment section(s) that apply to your patient:
    • Basic information about you
    • Patient name and date of birth
    • Information about the effects of the impairment(s)
  2. After completing Part B, sign the "Certification" section on the last page.

Providing information about the effects of the impairment

Because the severity of the functional limitations may vary from person to person within the same diagnostic category, all DTC applications are examined on a case-by-case basis based on the information you provide.

Examples of your patient’s abilities

Provide examples that describe your patient’s abilities in the applicable categories of impairment that are specific to your patient and include details of how they are limited in a particular area.

The year that your patient became impaired

The date should be the year when your patient’s limitations in that particular category became severe, not the year of diagnosis or the year the limitations began. Severe is defined as unable to or taking an inordinate amount of time all or substantially all of the time.

The limitations may be in more than one category of impairment, meaning there’s a cumulative effect based on significant limitations. In this case, the year should be when the limitations combined together, and were considered severe, such that they were unable to or took an inordinate amount of time all or substantially all of the time.

Life-sustaining therapy contains specific criteria.

Evaluate if your patient takes an inordinate amount of time

An inordinate amount of time is described as three times longer than another person of a similar age who does not have the impairment. The evaluation is based on the medical judgement of the medical practitioner.

Eligibility under mental functions

To be eligible for the DTC under limiting mental functions, a patient must have a severe and prolonged impairment, which meets specific criteria. DTC eligibility is not solely based on the presence of a medical condition or diagnosis, but rather on the effects of the impairment on a person’s ability to perform the mental functions necessary for everyday life.

Submit the application

If you used the reference number to complete the application online, it is automatically submitted to the CRA. Otherwise, you or the applicant must print and mail the paper form to the CRA. It is not mandatory to send in additional information with the application.

If we ask for more information

We may contact your office to verify the source of the application, request reports or ask for more information in order to assess an application. The letter we send will contain a reference number and instructions on what to do. If you are asked to submit a report please limit responses according to the limitations indicated.

We will send the applicant a notification when the review is complete and the decision has been made. For details, refer to CRA’s decision.

Get support from the CRA

The CRA has a dedicated toll-free phone line for medical practitioners who have questions about the DTC or who need help filling out the form. Call 1-800-280-2639 and our medical advisors, who are registered nurses, will get back to you promptly.
