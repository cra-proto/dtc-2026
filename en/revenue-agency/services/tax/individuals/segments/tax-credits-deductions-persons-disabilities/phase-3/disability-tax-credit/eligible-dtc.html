Who is eligible

You may be eligible for the DTC if you experience any of the following:

  • Have a medical condition on the list
  • Need life-sustaining therapy
  • Experience a limitation in physical or mental functions that is not a listed condition

Read about eligibility

If you have a medical condition on the list

One way to be eligible for the DTC is if a medical practitioner certifies that you have one of these specific severe, long-lasting medical conditions:

  • Alzheimer’s disease
  • Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (Lou Gehrig’s disease)
  • Angelman syndrome
  • Autism spectrum disorder, level 3
  • Blindness, bilateral (legally blind)
  • Cardiac functional class of 4 or an ejection fraction of 20% or less
  • Cerebral palsy (severe)
  • Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD), stage III or higher
  • Colostomy (permanent)
  • Cystic fibrosis
  • Dementia
  • Diabetes, type 1
  • Down syndrome (Trisomy 21)
  • Duchenne muscular dystrophy (advanced or severe)
  • Edwards syndrome (Trisomy 18)
  • Expressive aphasia, if sign language is your primary means of communicating
  • Hearing loss:
    • Severe or profound in both ears
    • Profound hearing loss in one ear and severe hearing loss in the other ear
  • Hemipelvectomy
  • Hemophilia A (severe)
  • Hip disarticulation
  • Huntington disease
  • Ileostomy (permanent)
  • Intellectual disability (severe, profound, or IQ of 70 or below)
  • Kidney (renal) failure requiring lifelong hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis
  • Limb amputation:
    • Lower (leg or foot)
    • Upper (trans carpal or higher)
  • Microcephaly
  • Paraplegia
  • Parkinson's disease (advanced or severe)
  • Patau syndrome (Trisomy 13)
  • Phenylketonuria (PKU)
  • Prader Willi syndrome
  • Progeria
  • Quadriplegia or tetraplegia
  • Relying only on lip-reading or sign language to understand conversations or communicate
  • Requiring supplemental oxygen (O2), if it is continuous and lifelong
  • Schizophrenia
  • Sickle cell disease (severe) requiring transfusions
  • Spinal muscular atrophy, type 1 and 2
  • Stroke (severe) with no functional recovery
  • Tay-Sachs disease (infantile/juvenile)
  • Total mutism
  • Traumatic brain injury (TBI) (severe)

If your condition is on the list, you can get ready to apply.

If you need life-sustaining therapy

Another way to be eligible for the DTC is if a medical practitioner certifies that you need regular, long-lasting therapy to keep you alive.

Life-sustaining therapy can include things like:

  • Dialysis
  • Insulin therapy
  • Oxygen therapy
  • Chest physiotherapy
  • Other types of therapies

Learn more: life-sustaining therapy eligibility

If you have a limitation in physical or mental function

Even if you are undiagnosed or have restrictions that are part of a medical diagnosis not on the list above, you can still be eligible if you have mental or physical limitations that impact your day-to-day life.

Determining eligibility based on physical or mental limitations

  1. Review the categories of limitations

    You can be eligible if you have a severe and prolonged impairment in 1 category or significant limitations in 2 or more categories. Select a category to see examples and read the details:

    • Mental function
    • Walking
    • Dressing
    • Speaking
    • Bowel or bladder function (eliminating)
    • Feeding
    • Hearing
    • Vision

  • Determine how you might qualify using the categories

    In one category

    To qualify with this method:

    • You are unable to do the activity, or it takes 3 times longer than someone of similar age without the impairment, even with the use of appropriate therapy, medication, and devices
    • This restriction is present all or almost all of the time (generally at least 90%)
    • The restriction has lasted or is expected to last for a continuous period of at least 12 months
    Combining categories

    If you do not meet the criteria to apply in a single category, you may be eligible if the combined effects of limitations from 2 or more categories significantly impact your day-to-day life.

    To qualify with this method:

    • The 2 or more limitations exist together all or almost all of the time (generally at least 90%)
    • If the limitations were combined, their combined impact (cumulative effect) would be both of the following:
      • Equivalent to being unable, or taking 3 times longer than someone of similar age without the impairment, to do an activity in one category
      • Present all or substantially all of the time (generally 90% or more) even with the use of appropriate therapy, devices, and medication

    Refer to: Cumulative effect

    • We determine eligibility only based on what your medical practitioner certifies.

    Getting the DTC does not depend on:

    •  If you can work or not
    •  Your age

    Eligibility for other provincial or federal disability payments

    Your eligibility for the DTC does not depend on other federal or provincial benefits you receive. If you receive Canada pension plan or Quebec pension plan disability benefits, workers’ compensation benefits, or other types of disability or insurance benefits, it does not necessarily mean that you are eligible for the DTC, because different programs have different criteria.

    However, if you are eligible for the DTC, you may be eligible for certain other payments.
